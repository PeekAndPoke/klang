# Motörhead trademark risk for consumer "Motör" naming — researched 2026-07-09

**Fact (this is trademark, not copyright).** Single words/short names can't be copyrighted; exposure is trademark only.

**Verified holdings.** **MOTÖRHEAD** wordmark owned by **The 2015 Kilmister Trust** (Lemmy's estate), US Reg. 4802001 /
Serial 86250450, filed 2014, covering earphones/headphones/earbuds/phone cases — **Class 9** (same class as downloadable
apps). Licensed via **Global Merchandising Services** (EU: Maurizio Distefano). Estate actively licenses digital/music
products: **NetEnt "Motörhead" slot** (2016), **Rock Band** + **Rocksmith** DLC. (Full register not pulled —
Justia/EUIPO
blocked direct access; treat as a floor.)

**Risk read.** The danger is NOT that anyone owns "motor" (it's a weak, near-descriptive common word → narrow
protection).
The danger is **"Motör" + the metal umlaut + a music product + app store (Class 9)** drawing a straight line to
Motörhead,
whose dominant distinctive root is also "Motör". Dominant-portion + related-goods analysis = real
likelihood-of-confusion
exposure, plus a well-resourced, litigious estate. **Acute practical threat for a solo founder = app-store takedown** on
an
IP complaint (Apple/Google pull fast, no court needed) → zeroes revenue overnight.

**The umlaut cuts against him:** it's decorative ("röck döts", Lemmy "to look mean") but it's Motörhead's most
distinctive
visual signature — reproducing it on a music product evokes the senior mark.

**Decision (Karsten, 2026-07-09):** consumer hits-app series renamed **"Motör Hits #N" → "Klang Hits #N"** to dodge the
vector. "Motör" retained only as the behind-glass engine metaphor (much lower risk — internal/dev-facing, not the
consumer
music-download product name). See [[brand-architecture-klang-motor]]. Standing guidance: get a trademark attorney before
spending money on any name.
